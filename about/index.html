About

Teaching, practice, and the questions in between

More than a decade in hotel operations, a teaching record across two university systems, and a research agenda built on what both revealed.

Background

I am a hospitality educator and researcher completing my PhD in Hospitality Management at Iowa State University, where I also teach as a graduate assistant. My work sits at the intersection of food safety compliance and hospitality workforce development, grounded in more than a decade of hotel front office management and years of teaching hospitality operations across lodging, foodservice, and marketing.

Before coming to the United States, I lectured at Takoradi Technical University in Ghana, teaching courses in lodging and front office operations, foodservice management, hospitality marketing, and food safety. That work continues to shape how I think about training, because the gap between a written procedure and what actually happens on a busy line looks similar in Takoradi and in Ames.

The question that pulled me toward research was a practical one. Food safety rules are written for an imagined average worker, and real kitchens are not staffed by that person. Understanding how managers train the people who fall outside that average, particularly employees with disabilities, turned out to be both a public health question and a workforce question at the same time.

Teaching

I have taught across two university systems, bringing hotel operations experience directly into the classroom. What I care about most in teaching is the same thing I study, which is whether the person in front of me can actually do the thing afterwards, not whether the material was covered.
