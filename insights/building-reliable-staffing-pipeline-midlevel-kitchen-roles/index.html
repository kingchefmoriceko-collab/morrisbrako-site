Building a Reliable Staffing Pipeline for Midlevel Kitchen Roles

A focused approach to developing a steady flow of qualified midlevel kitchen staff can improve service consistency and reduce turnover.

A persistent challenge in foodservice is the gap between entry-level positions and senior kitchen roles. Many establishments rely on a revolving door of short-term hires, which undermines team cohesion and quality control. I argue that creating a clear career pathway for cooks and sous chefs can address this gap.

First, employers should define the competencies required for each midlevel position and communicate them openly during recruitment. When candidates understand the skills they need to acquire, they can see a tangible progression rather than an ambiguous climb. This transparency also helps managers match training resources to specific development needs.

Second, structured mentorship programs connect experienced chefs with emerging talent. Regular, scheduled coaching sessions provide consistent feedback and allow the transfer of tacit knowledge that is rarely captured in manuals. I view mentorship as a cost-effective investment because it reduces the time needed for new staff to become fully productive.

Third, partnerships with local culinary schools and community colleges can create pipelines of ready-to-work graduates. By offering internships or apprenticeship slots that align with the defined competency framework, businesses can shape the skill set of future employees before they enter the full-time workforce.

Finally, retaining midlevel staff requires more than just training; it demands recognition of their contributions and opportunities for further advancement. When employees see a realistic path toward senior leadership, they are more likely to stay and invest in the organization’s success.
