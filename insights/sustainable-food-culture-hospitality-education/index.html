Sustainable Food Culture in Hospitality Education

I argue that embedding West African food traditions into hospitality curricula can deepen sustainability awareness while enriching student learning.

Sustainability in hospitality often focuses on energy use and waste reduction, yet the cultural dimension receives less attention. I contend that integrating West African food traditions offers a concrete way to illustrate sustainable sourcing, seasonal eating, and community engagement.

When students study dishes that rely on locally available grains, legumes, and spices, they see how culinary choices can support regional farmers and reduce transport emissions. This perspective also highlights the resilience of traditional food systems that have evolved to thrive in specific climates and economies.

Embedding such content does not require a complete redesign of existing courses. A module that examines the nutritional and environmental profiles of staple ingredients, paired with case studies of small-scale producers, can be woven into existing food safety or menu planning classes. The key is to present the material as a practical decision-making tool rather than an abstract concept.

Ultimately, I believe that exposing future hospitality leaders to diverse, sustainable food cultures prepares them to meet growing consumer demand for authenticity and responsibility. By valuing the lessons of West African culinary heritage, we broaden the definition of sustainability beyond the laboratory and into the everyday practices of kitchens worldwide.
