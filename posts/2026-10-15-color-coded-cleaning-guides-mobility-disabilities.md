---
title: "Color Coded Cleaning Guides for Employees With Mobility Disabilities"
date: 2026-10-15
summary: "Using color coded visual guides makes cleaning and sanitizing tasks accessible and reliable for employees with mobility disabilities."
---

![](images/headers/hygiene.svg)

Inclusive food safety training must address the practical realities of every worker. Cleaning and sanitizing stations are often designed for able-bodied staff, leaving employees with mobility disabilities to navigate high shelves, heavy tools, or awkward angles. When the task itself is a barrier, safety suffers for the whole kitchen.

A simple yet powerful solution is to create visual cleaning guides that use color coding to match each step with a specific tool or surface. A bright green sticker can indicate where a mop should be placed, a blue tag can mark the sanitizer dispenser, and a red label can signal the next area to be wiped. By attaching these pictorial cues at eye level and near the equipment, employees can follow the sequence without having to remember or reach for a written checklist.

This approach reduces the cognitive load of remembering multiple steps and eliminates the need to bend or stretch to read a sheet of paper. It also provides a universal reference that benefits all staff, especially during busy periods when quick verification is essential. The visual consistency builds confidence for employees with mobility challenges and reinforces consistent hygiene practices across the team.

I encourage managers to involve staff in designing the color scheme and placement of the guides, ensuring that the symbols are clear and the colors are distinct for everyone. Simple adjustments like these demonstrate a commitment to inclusion while strengthening overall food safety standards.
