---
title: "Video Modeling for Temperature Control with Employees With Intellectual Disabilities"
date: 2026-11-15
summary: "Using captioned video modeling makes thermometer use and temperature monitoring accessible for employees with intellectual disabilities."
---

![](images/headers/clarity.svg)

Ensuring that food is held at safe temperatures is a non-negotiable part of food safety, yet the steps involved in using a thermometer can be abstract for employees with intellectual disabilities. I find that the traditional written checklist often fails to convey the sequence of actions in a way that feels concrete and memorable.

Video modeling offers a visual narrative that shows each step in real time, from selecting the correct probe to recording the reading. When the video includes captions and optional narration in plain language, it provides both visual and textual cues that reinforce learning. Learners can pause, replay, and watch the same action multiple times until the process feels familiar.

A practical implementation starts with a short, focused video that isolates the temperature check task. The video should be broken into clearly labeled segments: preparing the thermometer, inserting the probe, waiting for the reading, and documenting the result. Captions should use simple sentences and avoid jargon. After viewing, the employee practices the task under supervision, using the video as a reference on a tablet or screen nearby.

Managers can support this approach by allocating time for video review during onboarding and by ensuring that the equipment is positioned so that the screen is easily visible from the work station. When employees can rely on a consistent visual guide, the likelihood of errors decreases and overall kitchen safety improves for the whole team.
