Research

Training that holds up on a real line

Evidence based frameworks connecting food safety policy to practical workforce training, so that operators have inclusive strategies that improve compliance and widen who can build a career in this industry.

Lines of inquiry

01

Inclusive training for employees with disabilities

How foodservice managers train employees with disabilities on food safety, what shapes their attitudes and practices, and what equitable, accessible training looks like on a working line.

02

Food safety compliance in commercial foodservice

How training materials and regulatory guidance can be made clearer and more usable, including natural language analysis of the documents workers are actually handed.

03

Careers and professional identity in hospitality education

Entrepreneurship, career development, curriculum alignment, and how students form professional identities as they enter the industry.

04

Food culture and sustainability in West Africa

Culinary heritage, food practices, oil quality and safety, and sustainable foodservice in West African contexts.

Doctoral dissertation

Assessing food safety training for individuals with disabilities in the US foodservice industry

A national mixed methods study of 315 foodservice managers, grounded in the Knowledge, Attitudes, and Practices framework. Advised by Dr. Anirudh R. Naig at Iowa State University.

In progress, findings shared as they clear publication

While the dissertation is in progress, I write about the practical side of this work in Insights, and post a short training idea each day on the home page.
